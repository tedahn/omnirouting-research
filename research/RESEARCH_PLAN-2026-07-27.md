# Research plan: omni routing and dynamic model selection landscape

- **Status:** Ready for bounded execution after owner confirmation
- **Prepared:** 2026-07-27
- **Owner:** Research lead (identity unresolved)
- **Decision:** Which routing directions merit deeper investigation, monitoring, or representative experimentation?
- **Default time scope:** 2023–present; extend earlier only for foundational methods
- **Geography:** Global, public evidence

## Desired outcome

Produce an auditable map of companies exploring omni routing or dynamic model selection, the methods they disclose, the outcomes actually studied, the limits of those outcomes, and plausible future directions. The result should support research prioritization—not a generic vendor ranking or purchase recommendation.

## Stage gates

| Stage | Work | Required output | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Frame | Confirm definitions, decision, owner, scope, budget, and exclusion rules | Approved brief and methodology | Owner accepts the research boundary |
| 1. Discover | Build a broad company/project universe from primary and high-quality discovery sources | `company-landscape.csv` candidates and search log | Two passes add no material method category or budget stops |
| 2. Profile | Create source-grounded company profiles and reject marketing-only candidates | Profile files, claims, and sources | Every included company has a qualifying primary artifact |
| 3. Classify | Map disclosed mechanisms into a common routing taxonomy | `methodologies.csv` | Unknown fields and category disagreements are explicit |
| 4. Normalize outcomes | Extract evaluation design, metrics, baselines, results, and limitations | `outcomes.csv` | No comparison hides incompatible settings or missing data |
| 5. Challenge | Search for contradictions, negative results, non-adoption, lock-in, and operational constraints | Countercase section and exclusions | Strongest credible alternative explanation is represented |
| 6. Forecast | Build calibrated scenarios from mechanisms, drivers, barriers, and leading indicators | `future-scenarios.csv` | Forecasts have falsifiers and resolution rules |
| 7. Decide | Rank research directions by evidence value and decision relevance | Decision memo or experiment backlog | Each recommendation has an owner, stop rule, and reversal evidence |

## Discovery strategy

Search by architecture and problem, not only by the phrase “omni routing.” Cover model gateways, provider/model routers, cascades, learned selectors, orchestration platforms, evaluation systems, inference infrastructure, and policy/governance routing. Search official documentation, engineering publications, repositories, papers, conference talks, model/system cards, and named builder material before relying on commentary.

Maintain separate discovery queries for quality optimization, cost/latency constraints, reliability/fallback, multimodal selection, policy/data residency, capacity/load, and adaptive learning. Log searches that produce no useful evidence to prevent repeated work.

## Analysis workstreams

### A. Company landscape

Identify entity type, relevant product/project, routing boundary, disclosed maturity, primary artifacts, production evidence, constraints, and commercial incentives. Keep a company in the universe even when it becomes an exclusion or counterexample.

### B. Methodology taxonomy

Compare decision locus, candidate set, signals, mechanism, objective, learning mode, execution pattern, fallback, feedback loop, and known failure modes. Treat a company's marketing label as metadata, not the taxonomy authority.

### C. Outcome evidence

Separate claimed, experimentally observed, independently replicated, and production-observed outcomes. Normalize only compatible metrics and preserve workload, baseline, evaluator, sample, cost, latency, quality, safety, and operational context.

### D. Future direction

Forecast mechanisms and constraints rather than company winners. Examine convergence, vertical specialization, evaluation-driven routing, online adaptation, multimodal/agent routing, governance constraints, model commoditization, switching costs, and provider incentives as hypotheses to test—not conclusions.

## Evidence thresholds

- **Company inclusion:** at least one attributable primary artifact.
- **Method established:** the mechanism is described precisely enough to classify and distinguish from alternatives.
- **Outcome demonstrated:** metric, baseline, workload, evaluation setting, result, and limitations are available.
- **Cross-company conclusion:** at least two independent sources or cases with compatible scope, plus contradiction search.
- **Future scenario:** mechanism, evidence anchors, probability/range, leading indicators, falsifiers, and resolution rule.

Anything below its threshold remains a lead, source claim, Looks believable, Overstated, or Unknown—not a fact.

## Initial deliverables

1. Company universe and inclusion/exclusion log.
2. Ten to fifteen stratified profiles, adjusted to the evidence universe rather than fixed in advance.
3. Methodology matrix and architecture map.
4. Outcome evidence matrix with non-comparability warnings.
5. Countercase and negative-evidence memo.
6. Two-horizon future scenario register.
7. Prioritized follow-up questions and representative experiment backlog.

## Stop rules

Stop or narrow when the research no longer changes prioritization, primary evidence is unavailable for a category, two discovery passes add no material category, comparable outcome evidence is too sparse, the approved budget is reached, or legal/privacy boundaries would be crossed. State residual uncertainty instead of filling it with confident synthesis.
