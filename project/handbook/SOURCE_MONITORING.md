# Source monitoring

Monitor claims, releases, and corrections—not personalities as brands. Start with official documentation and runtime evidence, then use named practitioners to discover hypotheses worth testing.

## Seed watchlist

- Official vendor documentation, release notes, system/model cards, repositories, papers, status pages, and help centers.
- Researchers and builders with direct knowledge of the work they discuss.
- Independent evaluators who publish methods, fixtures, and failures.
- Community sources such as Andrej Karpathy, Boris Cherny, and TokenMaxxing only as claim-level inputs. Their names do not preassign credibility or intent.

Add or remove sources based on evidence value, coverage, correction behavior, and decision relevance. Record disclosed affiliations and commercial incentives without speculating about undisclosed motives.

## Cadence

- **Release-triggered:** verify the official announcement, affected surfaces, access, changed limits, safety notes, and migration details; then rerun relevant evaluations.
- **Weekly:** triage new high-impact claims, log exact statements and sources, seek primary evidence and contradiction, and assign owners.
- **Monthly:** resolve matured claims, update source-by-domain scorecards, prune low-value feeds, and inspect stale decisions or techniques.
- **Quarterly:** audit watchlist coverage, scoring calibration, false positives, blind spots, and whether monitoring changed any decision.

## Intake rule

For each claim, capture the smallest faithful statement, publication and access dates, domain, claim type, knowledge proximity, evidence links, disclosed incentives, counterevidence, decision relevance, and resolution rule. Label promotional framing separately from factual content. Do not amplify an exciting claim before its evidence state is visible.
